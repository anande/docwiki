    Mathematics Relearning Roadmap: From Basics to Mastery¶

    A structured 7-year plan to go from forgotten school math to graduate-level mathematics, olympiad problem-solving, and research readiness

    Commitment: 1 hour/day | Timeline: 7+ years | Designed for full-time professionals

    How to Use This Plan¶

    This roadmap is built for someone who studied engineering 20 years ago and wants to restart mathematics from scratch -- progressing all the way to PhD-level topics, olympiad problem-solving, and International Congress of Mathematicians (ICM) territory.

    Reality Check¶

    • Available time: ~1 hour/day = ~7 hours/week = ~365 hours/year
    • 365 hours/year is roughly one university semester of focused math study
    • Timeline: 7+ years to reach advanced/research level -- this is a marathon, not a sprint
    • Key principle: Consistency beats intensity. 1 hour every day trumps 7 hours on Sunday.

    Daily Practice Structure (1 hour)¶

    Segment Duration Activity
    Warm-up 10 min Review yesterday's concepts, do 2-3 quick problems
    New material 25 min Read/watch new topic, take notes
    Problem solving 20 min Work through exercises (start easy, increase difficulty)
    Reflection 5 min Write down what you learned, note any confusion

    YEAR 1: Foundations Revival (School Math)¶

    Quarter 1 (Months 1-3): Arithmetic, Number Sense, Basic Algebra¶

    Topics:

    • Number systems (natural, whole, integers, rationals, irrationals, reals)
    • Order of operations, fractions, decimals, percentages
    • Exponents and radicals (laws of indices)
    • Algebraic expressions, factoring, expanding
    • Linear equations and inequalities
    • Quadratic equations (factoring, completing the square, formula)
    • Word problems and mathematical modeling

    Resources¶

    Resource Type Resource Notes
    Book Basic Mathematics by Serge Lang The single best "start from scratch" book by a legendary mathematician
    Book (alt) Algebra by I.M. Gelfand Elegant, problem-driven, written for self-study
    YouTube Khan Academy Algebra Complete, free, well-structured
    YouTube Professor Leonard -- Intermediate Algebra Full course, thorough
    Practice Khan Academy exercises Instant feedback
    Website AoPS Alcumus Adaptive problem practice

    Monthly Targets¶

    • Month 1: Arithmetic fluency, fractions/decimals/percentages, basic exponents
    • Month 2: Algebraic manipulation, linear equations, word problems
    • Month 3: Quadratics, systems of equations, inequalities

    Milestone

    Solve any school-level algebra problem confidently.

    Quarter 2 (Months 4-6): Geometry and Coordinate Geometry¶

    Topics:

    • Euclidean geometry: angles, triangles, circles, polygons
    • Congruence and similarity theorems (SSS, SAS, ASA, AA)
    • Pythagorean theorem and applications
    • Area and volume formulas (derivations, not just memorization)
    • Coordinate geometry: distance, midpoint, slope, equation of lines
    • Conic sections basics (circle, parabola, ellipse, hyperbola)

    Resources¶

    Resource Type Resource Notes
    Book Geometry by I.M. Gelfand & T. Saul Problem-driven, builds intuition
    Book Euclid's Elements Free online -- read Book I for axiomatic reasoning
    YouTube 3Blue1Brown -- Lockdown Math Geometry intuition
    YouTube Khan Academy Geometry Full playlist

    Monthly Targets¶

    • Month 4: Angles, triangles, congruence/similarity proofs
    • Month 5: Circles (theorems, tangents, chords), polygons, area/volume
    • Month 6: Coordinate geometry, conic sections, transformation geometry

    Milestone

    Write basic geometric proofs. Solve coordinate geometry problems.

    Quarter 3 (Months 7-9): Trigonometry¶

    Topics:

    • Trigonometric ratios (sin, cos, tan) and their reciprocals
    • Unit circle, radians vs. degrees
    • Trigonometric identities (Pythagorean, sum/difference, double angle, half angle)
    • Graphs of trigonometric functions, amplitude, period, phase shift
    • Inverse trigonometric functions
    • Solving trigonometric equations
    • Applications: heights & distances, wave functions

    Resources¶

    Resource Type Resource Notes
    Book Trigonometry by I.M. Gelfand & M. Saul Definitive self-study trig book
    Book Precalculus Mathematics in a Nutshell by George Simmons Concise, excellent review
    YouTube Professor Leonard -- Trigonometry Full course

    Interactive Desmos Graph trig functions interactively

    Monthly Targets¶

    • Month 7: Trig ratios, unit circle, basic identities
    • Month 8: Advanced identities, inverse trig, solving equations
    • Month 9: Applications, graphs, review entire Year 1 Q1-Q3

    Milestone

    Prove trigonometric identities. Convert fluently between degrees and radians.

    Quarter 4 (Months 10-12): Pre-Calculus and Functions¶

    Topics:

    • Functions: domain, range, composition, inverses
    • Polynomial functions, rational functions
    • Exponential and logarithmic functions (laws of logarithms)
    • Sequences and series (arithmetic, geometric)
    • Summation notation, mathematical induction (first exposure)
    • Binomial theorem
    • Limits (intuitive introduction)

    Resources¶

    Resource Type Resource Notes
    Book Precalculus Mathematics in a Nutshell by George Simmons Concise, covers everything
    Book Functions and Graphs by I.M. Gelfand Short, brilliant
    YouTube Khan Academy Precalculus Full playlist
    YouTube Organic Chemistry Tutor -- Precalculus Worked examples

    Monthly Targets¶

    • Month 10: Functions deeply -- types, transformations, composition, inverses
    • Month 11: Exponentials, logarithms, sequences, series
    • Month 12: Binomial theorem, mathematical induction, intro to limits

    Milestone

    Comfortable with function manipulation. Ready for calculus.

    YEAR 2: Calculus and Core Tools¶

    Quarter 1 (Months 1-3): Single Variable Calculus -- Differentiation¶

    Topics:

    • Limits and continuity (epsilon-delta definition -- conceptual understanding)
    • Definition of derivative, first principles
    • Differentiation rules (power, product, quotient, chain)
    • Implicit differentiation, logarithmic differentiation
    • Applications: rates of change, maxima/minima, curve sketching
    • Mean Value Theorem, L'Hopital's Rule
    • Related rates, optimization problems

    Resources¶

    Resource Type Resource Notes
    Book Calculus by James Stewart The standard, comprehensive, lots of exercises
    Book (rigorous) Calculus by Michael Spivak Beautiful, proof-based; save for re-read in Year 3
    YouTube 3Blue1Brown -- Essence of Calculus Watch this first -- 19 videos, stunning visual intuition
    YouTube Professor Leonard -- Calculus 1 Long but thorough
    YouTube MIT OCW 18.01 Denis Auroux or David Jerison

    Monthly Targets¶

    • Month 1: Limits, continuity, derivative definition and basic rules
    • Month 2: Chain rule, implicit differentiation, applications (maxima/minima)
    • Month 3: Related rates, optimization, curve sketching, L'Hopital's

    Milestone

    Differentiate any standard function. Solve optimization problems.

    Quarter 2 (Months 4-6): Single Variable Calculus -- Integration¶

    Topics:

    • Definite and indefinite integrals, Fundamental Theorem of Calculus
    • Integration techniques: substitution, integration by parts, partial fractions, trigonometric substitution
    • Improper integrals
    • Applications: area under curves, volumes of revolution (disk, shell, washer)
    • Arc length, surface area
    • Sequences and series revisited: convergence tests
    • Taylor and Maclaurin series

    Resources¶

    Resource Type Resource Notes
    Book Same as Q1 (Stewart or Spivak) Continue with the same textbook
    YouTube Professor Leonard -- Calculus 2 Full integration course
    YouTube Trefor Bazett -- Calculus Clear, modern
    Practice Paul's Online Math Notes Excellent free practice with solutions

    Monthly Targets¶

    • Month 4: Fundamental Theorem, basic integration, substitution
    • Month 5: Integration by parts, partial fractions, trig substitution, applications
    • Month 6: Sequences, series, convergence tests, Taylor series

    Milestone

    Integrate using all standard techniques. Understand series convergence.

    Quarter 3 (Months 7-9): Multivariable Calculus¶

    Topics:

    • Vectors in 2D and 3D, dot product, cross product
    • Partial derivatives, gradients, directional derivatives
    • Double integrals, triple integrals, change of variables (Jacobian)
    • Polar, cylindrical, spherical coordinates
    • Line integrals, surface integrals
    • Green's Theorem, Stokes' Theorem, Divergence Theorem (conceptual)

    Resources¶

    • Month 7: Vectors, partial derivatives, gradients
    • Month 8: Double and triple integrals, coordinate transformations
    • Month 9: Line/surface integrals, fundamental theorems of vector calculus

    Milestone

    Set up and evaluate double/triple integrals. Understand vector calculus theorems.

    Quarter 4 (Months 10-12): Linear Algebra¶

    Topics:

    • Vectors, vector spaces, subspaces
    • Matrices: operations, row reduction, echelon forms
    • Systems of linear equations
    • Determinants
    • Eigenvalues and eigenvectors
    • Linear transformations
    • Inner product spaces, orthogonality, Gram-Schmidt
    • Singular Value Decomposition (conceptual)

    • Month 10: Vector spaces, matrices, systems of equations, row reduction
    • Month 11: Determinants, eigenvalues/eigenvectors, diagonalization
    • Month 12: Linear transformations, orthogonality, SVD intro

    Milestone

    Solve eigenvalue problems. Understand geometric meaning of matrix operations.

    YEAR 3: Probability, Statistics, and Proof Transition¶

    Quarter 1 (Months 1-3): Probability and Statistics¶

    Topics:

    • Combinatorics: permutations, combinations, binomial coefficients
    • Probability axioms, conditional probability, Bayes' theorem
    • Random variables (discrete and continuous), expectation, variance
    • Common distributions: binomial, Poisson, normal, exponential
    • Law of Large Numbers, Central Limit Theorem
    • Hypothesis testing, confidence intervals (conceptual)
    • Markov chains (introduction)

    Resources¶

    Resource Type Resource Notes
    Book Introduction to Probability by Bertsekas & Tsitsiklis Clear, well-structured

    Monthly Targets¶

    • Month 1: Combinatorics, probability axioms, conditional probability, Bayes
    • Month 2: Random variables, distributions, expectation, variance
    • Month 3: LLN, CLT, hypothesis testing basics, Markov chains intro

    Quarter 2 (Months 4-6): Differential Equations¶

    Topics:

    • First-order ODEs: separable, linear, exact, integrating factors
    • Second-order linear ODEs: constant coefficients, undetermined coefficients, variation of parameters
    • Systems of ODEs, phase portraits
    • Laplace transforms
    • Introduction to PDEs (heat equation, wave equation -- conceptual)
    • Fourier series (introduction)

    Resources¶

    Resource Type Resource Notes

    • Month 5: Second-order ODEs, systems of ODEs
    • Month 6: Laplace transforms, Fourier series intro, PDE concepts

    Quarter 3-4 (Months 7-12): Learning to Write Proofs + Discrete Mathematics¶

    Critical Transition

    This is the single most important skill transition in the entire roadmap -- from doing math (computation) to proving math (rigorous reasoning). Do not rush this section.

    Topics:

    • Logic: propositions, quantifiers, truth tables
    • Proof techniques: direct proof, contrapositive, contradiction, induction (strong and weak)
    • Set theory: unions, intersections, power sets, Cartesian products
    • Relations: equivalence relations, partial orders
    • Functions: injective, surjective, bijective, cardinality
    • Countability, Cantor's diagonal argument
    • Graph theory basics, combinatorics revisited with proofs
    • Number theory: divisibility, primes, modular arithmetic, Fermat's little theorem, Euler's theorem

    Resources¶

    Resource Type Resource Notes
    Book How to Prove It by Daniel Velleman Essential transition book from computation to proof
    Book (free) Book of Proof by Richard Hammack Free online
    Book Discrete Mathematics and Its Applications by Kenneth Rosen Comprehensive
    Book (number theory) Elementary Number Theory by David Burton Accessible intro
    YouTube MIT OCW 6.042J Mathematics for Computer Science
    YouTube Trefor Bazett -- Discrete Math Clear playlist

    Monthly Targets¶

    • Month 7-8: Logic, proof techniques (direct, contrapositive, contradiction, induction)
    • Month 9-10: Set theory, relations, functions, cardinality
    • Month 11-12: Number theory, graph theory, combinatorial proofs

    Milestone

    Write clean mathematical proofs. You have crossed the bridge from calculation to reasoning.

    YEAR 4: Undergraduate Pure Mathematics¶

    Semester 1 (Months 1-6): Real Analysis¶

    This is where mathematics transforms. You move from calculating to reasoning rigorously about why calculus works.

    Topics:

    • The real number system, completeness axiom, supremum/infimum
    • Sequences: convergence, Cauchy sequences, Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem
    • Series: absolute vs conditional convergence, rearrangements
    • Topology of the real line: open/closed sets, compactness, connectedness
    • Continuity: epsilon-delta, uniform continuity, Intermediate Value Theorem (rigorous proof)
    • Differentiability: Mean Value Theorem (rigorous), Taylor's theorem
    • Riemann integral: definition, properties, Fundamental Theorem of Calculus (rigorous)
    • Sequences and series of functions, uniform convergence

    Resources¶

    Resource Type Resource Notes
    Book Understanding Analysis by Stephen Abbott The best first real analysis book, readable, well-motivated
    Book (harder) Principles of Mathematical Analysis ("Baby Rudin") by Walter Rudin Terse, elegant, challenging; attempt after Abbott
    YouTube Francis Su -- Real Analysis (Harvey Mudd) Warm, clear, motivating
    YouTube The Bright Side of Mathematics -- Real Analysis Excellent playlist
    Blog Terry Tao's blog Start reading his expository posts

    Monthly Targets¶

    • Month 1: Real numbers, completeness, sequences
    • Month 2: Series, topology of the real line
    • Month 3: Continuity (epsilon-delta proofs)
    • Month 4: Differentiation, Mean Value Theorem
    • Month 5: Riemann integration
    • Month 6: Sequences/series of functions, uniform convergence

    Milestone

    Prove the Intermediate Value Theorem. Understand why epsilon-delta matters.

    Semester 2 (Months 7-12): Abstract Algebra¶

    Topics:

    • Groups: definition, subgroups, cyclic groups, permutation groups
    • Cosets, Lagrange's theorem, normal subgroups, quotient groups
    • Group homomorphisms, isomorphism theorems
    • Rings: definition, ideals, quotient rings, polynomial rings
    • Fields: definition, field extensions (introduction)
    • Introduction to Galois Theory (conceptual -- why fifth-degree polynomials have no general formula)

    Resources¶

    Resource Type Resource Notes
    Book Algebra by Michael Artin Geometric intuition, well-written, broad
    Book (encyclopedic) Abstract Algebra by Dummit & Foote Comprehensive reference
    Book (gentle) A Book of Abstract Algebra by Charles Pinter Dover, affordable, self-study friendly
    YouTube Socratica -- Abstract Algebra Short, clear videos
    YouTube Visual Group Theory by Nathan Carter Lectures based on his book
    YouTube Michael Penn -- Algebra Problem walkthroughs

    Monthly Targets¶

    • Month 7-8: Groups, subgroups, cyclic groups, Lagrange's theorem
    • Month 9-10: Normal subgroups, quotient groups, homomorphisms, isomorphism theorems
    • Month 11-12: Rings, ideals, fields, glimpse of Galois theory

    Milestone

    Prove Lagrange's theorem. Understand why groups capture symmetry.

    YEAR 5: Topology, Complex Analysis, and Problem-Solving¶

    Semester 1 (Months 1-6): Topology + Complex Analysis¶

    Topics:

    • Topological spaces, open/closed sets, bases
    • Continuity in topological setting
    • Connectedness, compactness (Heine-Borel, Bolzano-Weierstrass generalized)
    • Product topology, quotient topology
    • Introduction to fundamental group (if time permits)

    Resources:

    Resource Type Resource Notes
    Book Topology by James Munkres The standard, readable, comprehensive
    Book (shorter) Introduction to Topology by Bert Mendelson Dover
    YouTube The Bright Side of Mathematics -- Topology Clear playlist

    Topics:

    • Complex numbers: algebra, geometry, polar form, Euler's formula
    • Analytic functions, Cauchy-Riemann equations
    • Complex integration, Cauchy's integral theorem and formula
    • Taylor and Laurent series in the complex plane
    • Residues and the Residue Theorem
    • Applications: evaluating real integrals using contour integration

    Resources:

    Resource Type Resource Notes

    Semester 2 (Months 7-12): Olympiad Problem Solving¶

    This track builds competition-style problem-solving muscle -- a different skill from proof-writing that strengthens mathematical intuition enormously.

    Topics:

    • Algebraic inequalities (AM-GM, Cauchy-Schwarz, Jensen's, Schur's)
    • Combinatorial arguments, pigeonhole principle, extremal principles
    • Number theory: congruences, Legendre symbol, quadratic reciprocity
    • Geometry: advanced Euclidean, projective, inversive geometry
    • Functional equations
    • Generating functions
    • Problem-solving strategies: invariants, monovariants, coloring arguments

    • Month 9-10: Number theory problems, combinatorics problems
    • Month 11-12: Geometry problems, functional equations

    Milestone

    Solve problems from national olympiad qualifying rounds (e.g., AMC 12, AIME level).

    YEAR 6: Graduate-Level Mathematics¶

    Semester 1 (Months 1-6): Measure Theory and Functional Analysis¶

    Topics:

    • Sigma-algebras, measures, Lebesgue measure
    • Measurable functions, Lebesgue integral
    • Convergence theorems (Monotone, Dominated, Fatou's Lemma)
    • ( L^p ) spaces
    • Product measures, Fubini's theorem

    Resources:

    Resource Type Resource Notes

    Topics:

    • Normed spaces, Banach spaces, Hilbert spaces
    • Bounded linear operators
    • Hahn-Banach theorem, Open Mapping theorem, Closed Graph theorem
    • Spectral theory (introduction)

    Resources:

    Resource Type Resource Notes

    Semester 2 (Months 7-12): Choose Your Adventure (Specialization)¶

    At this point, follow your curiosity. Pick one or two areas to dive deep:

    • Book: Algebraic Number Theory by Neukirch
    • Connects to Fermat's Last Theorem, modern number theory
    • Book: Introduction to Smooth Manifolds by John Lee
    • Book: Riemannian Geometry by do Carmo
    • Connects to general relativity, modern physics
    • Book: Algebraic Topology by Allen Hatcher (free online)
    • Fundamental groups, homology, cohomology
    • Book: Partial Differential Equations by Lawrence Evans
    • Connects to physics, applied math, fluid dynamics
    • Book: A Mathematical Introduction to Logic by Enderton
    • Godel's incompleteness theorems, foundations of mathematics

    YEAR 7+: Research-Level Exploration¶

    • Read survey articles and expository papers (AMS Notices, Bulletin of the AMS)
    • Follow Fields Medal lectures on YouTube (ICM recordings)
    • Read Terry Tao's blog regularly
    • Engage with the math community: Math StackExchange, MathOverflow
    • Consider: Open University courses, audit graduate courses online
    • Attend local math meetups or seminars if available
    • Work through unsolved problems in your chosen specialty
    • Consider writing up your own solutions/expositions

    Bridge to Quantum Computing (Parallel Track)¶

    There is a remarkably natural path from pure mathematics to quantum computing. Quantum mechanics -- and by extension quantum computing -- is essentially linear algebra over complex vector spaces with a probabilistic interpretation. Your math roadmap already builds every prerequisite; the question is simply when to start.

    When to Start: The Parallel Track¶

    Start QC from Year 3 onward

    After completing linear algebra (Year 2 Q4) and probability (Year 3 Q1), you have enough math to begin quantum computing at the introductory level. You do not need to wait until Year 6.

    Recommended approach: Dedicate one weekend session per month (2-3 hours) to quantum computing starting in Year 3, while your weekday hour stays on the main math track. As your math deepens, your quantum computing understanding deepens in lockstep.

    QC Level 1 (Year 3): Qubits, Gates, and Circuits¶

    Start after: Linear Algebra + Complex Numbers basics

    Topics:

    • What is a qubit? Superposition, the Bloch sphere
    • Quantum gates as unitary matrices (Pauli X/Y/Z, Hadamard, CNOT, Toffoli)
    • Quantum circuits: sequential gate application
    • Measurement and the Born rule
    • Bell states and entanglement
    • No-cloning theorem

    Resources¶

    Resource Type Resource Notes

    YouTube IBM Qiskit -- Introduction to Quantum Computing Hands-on from Day 1
    Book Quantum Computing: An Applied Approach by Jack Hidary Practical, code-included

    Monthly Targets (one weekend session/month)¶

    • Month 1-2: Qubits, superposition, measurement, Bloch sphere
    • Month 3-4: Single-qubit gates (H, X, Y, Z, S, T), matrix representation
    • Month 5-6: Multi-qubit gates (CNOT, Toffoli), tensor products, Bell states

    Milestone

    Build and simulate a quantum circuit. Understand why a qubit is a unit vector in ( \mathbb{C}^2 ).

    QC Level 2 (Year 4): Quantum Algorithms¶

    Start after: Probability + Proof-writing

    Topics:

    • Quantum parallelism and interference
    • Deutsch-Jozsa algorithm (toy, but foundational)
    • Grover's search algorithm (quadratic speedup)
    • Quantum Fourier Transform
    • Shor's algorithm (integer factorization -- the "killer app")
    • Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) -- near-term algorithms
    • Quantum simulation

    Monthly Targets (one weekend session/month)¶

    • Month 1-3: Deutsch-Jozsa, Bernstein-Vazirani, Simon's algorithm
    • Month 4-6: Grover's algorithm, amplitude amplification
    • Month 7-9: Quantum Fourier Transform, phase estimation
    • Month 10-12: Shor's algorithm, VQE basics

    Milestone

    Implement Shor's algorithm in Qiskit on a simulator. Explain why it achieves exponential speedup.

    QC Level 3 (Year 5-6): Quantum Error Correction and Information Theory¶

    Start after: Abstract Algebra (groups) + Topology basics

    Topics:

    • Quantum noise, decoherence, density matrices
    • Quantum error correction codes (Shor code, Steane code, surface codes)
    • Stabilizer formalism (uses group theory heavily)
    • Quantum information theory: von Neumann entropy, quantum channels
    • Fault-tolerant quantum computation
    • Topological quantum computing (connections to topology from Year 5)

    QC Level 4 (Year 7+): Research Frontiers¶

    Runs in parallel with your math specialization

    At this stage, quantum computing and pure mathematics begin to merge. Possible directions:

    • Computational complexity classes: BQP, QMA, PostBQP
    • Quantum-classical separations
    • Book: Quantum Computing Since Democritus by Scott Aaronson -- brilliant, opinionated, accessible
    • Resource: Complexity Zoo
    • Entanglement measures, Bell inequalities, CHSH games
    • Quantum cryptography (BB84, QKD)
    • Connections to operator algebras and functional analysis
    • Book: From Classical to Quantum Shannon Theory by Mark Wilde (free online)
    • Anyons, braiding, topological order
    • Deep connections to algebraic topology and knot theory
    • Book: Introduction to Topological Quantum Computation by Jiannis Pachos
    • Perfect extension of Year 5 topology + Year 4 group theory
    • Quantum kernel methods, variational circuits
    • Barren plateaus, expressibility
    • Resource: PennyLane QML tutorials
    • Book: Supervised Learning with Quantum Computers by Schuld & Petruccione

    Weekly / Monthly / Yearly Review Cadence¶

    Weekly (Every Sunday, 30 min)¶

    • Review the week's notes
    • Attempt 3-5 problems from the current topic without looking at solutions
    • Rate your confidence (1-5) on each sub-topic covered
    • Adjust pace: if below 3, slow down; if above 4, push harder

    Monthly (Last day of each month, 1 hour)¶

    • Take a "monthly quiz" -- 10 problems covering the month's material
    • Review your problem-solving journal: patterns in mistakes?
    • Read one article from Quanta Magazine or one blog post from Terry Tao
    • Update your learning log

    Yearly (End of December, 2 hours)¶

    • Comprehensive review of the year's topics
    • Attempt a timed problem set (past AMC/AIME paper after Year 2, Putnam after Year 4)
    • Set goals for the next year
    • Celebrate your progress -- keep a "math journal" showing your first clumsy proofs vs. your latest

    Practical Tips for Sustainability¶

    1. Use a physical notebook for problem-solving. Writing math by hand activates different cognitive pathways than typing.
    2. Keep a "stuck" journal. When you cannot solve a problem, write down what you tried. Come back to it a week later.
    3. Interleave topics when bored. If algebra feels stale, do geometry for a few days.
    4. Teach what you learn. Write blog posts, explain to friends, or post solutions on Math StackExchange.
    5. Do not skip exercises. Reading math without doing problems is like watching swimming videos without getting in the pool.
    6. Embrace being stuck. Spending 30 minutes on a problem you cannot solve is more valuable than solving 10 easy problems.
    7. Find a study buddy or join an online community (AoPS forums, Reddit r/learnmath, Math StackExchange).
    8. Adjust the plan. This is a guide, not a prison. If you finish a topic early, move on. If you need more time, take it.
